What I've built

2026.07 — Co-founder

Good Neighbor

Good Neighbor is a marketplace where verified teens do small paid jobs for neighbors in New York City. I started it with a friend in July, and I built the actual app myself in September using AI coding agents.

The whole thing runs on one idea: an adult should always be one step away from what a teen is doing.

Install
Web app that installs straight to the home screen. No app store.
Verification
A school-ID photo plus a live selfie, checked before anyone can work.
Parent oversight
A parent sits on every teen account and approves jobs by default.
Location
Teens see a fuzzed pin and a neighborhood, nothing more, until a job is confirmed. Then the exact address unlocks for that one teen, and it drops out of their history once the job's done.
Exit
Either side, or the teen's parent, can cancel any time before the job is finished. No reason required.
Testing
56 automated tests, including tests that check safety at the database level, not just in the app.
Job rules
Written from federal and New York child-labor guidelines. No ladders, roofs, gutters, driving, or power tools. An adult is home, or the job stays outside.
Not launched yet

My dad set up the Apple Developer account and we've submitted the app; now we're waiting on Apple's review. Before any teen does a real job, I'm confirming New York's working-paper rules with the state Department of Labor. I haven't made any money from it yet. What I've learned so far is that posting consistently matters, and that getting something all the way to the App Store is harder than building it.

2026.07 — Market research

Market research for a venture investor

I've been doing contract research for a venture investor building a high-end home-maintenance business. I built a target list of 49 ZIP codes, tracking income, home values, ownership rates, and named competitors for each one, then narrowed it to a top 20 across Westchester and northern New Jersey.

From there I scoped a survey of homeowners with $5 million-plus homes: contacted ten research firms, ran a 28-question review to pick one, and negotiated the billing so the client only pays for responses that actually come in.
